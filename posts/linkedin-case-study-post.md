In my last post, I said the first case study was in the oven. It's out.

Knowledge Automata is an open-source project I built in my personal time, over two weekends, some late nights and a fair amount of coffee. AI agents turn the raw notes a group produces into validated records, each traceable to its source. It runs on @Cohere, and its docs are open, so my agents could read them directly.

It was the first real test of my 16 principles. I didn't write prompts or instruction files for my agents. I just pointed them at the principles repo. What grew from there was so organic: a way of working that kept improving itself, with every lesson written down for the next session. The side effect that surprised me most: my agents kept me closer to my own principles.

At the start of a project, everyone agrees on good practice. Two weeks later, busy with the work, we forget. Attention is limited. My agents reread the rules every time, in seconds. When I drifted, they pushed back: that's out of scope, according to the backlog. Each time, I'd already said it. I'd just forgotten.

Reading is remembering.

Same four pillars as last time, now with real examples:
Think before you generate: the backlog became a contract.
Generate responsibly: review went every direction. AI reviewed AI, I reviewed the AI, and the AI reviewed me.
Prove it works: assumptions were labeled "not proven yet" until we tested them.
Build for the future: core docs, written once, for humans and AIs alike.

None of that is new. What's changed is what it costs to follow it.

What failed, failed where systems meet: a line I missed in an API's docs, and a file a tool said it saved but didn't. Agents move faster than people can absorb, so my next experiment is to slow down at the boundaries.

In my first post I asked: how much engineering discipline can we afford? Turns out, quite a lot. Alone, as a robotics engineer and not a traditional software developer, I'd probably still be setting up the repo.

Want to try it? Point your agents at the principles repo, and let the cooking begin.
