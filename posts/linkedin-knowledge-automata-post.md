In my last post, I said the first case study was in the oven. It's out.

Knowledge Automata is an open-source project I built in my personal time, over two weekends, some late nights and a fair amount of coffee. AI agents turn the raw notes a group produces into validated records, each traceable to its source. It runs on @Cohere, and its docs are open, so my agents could read them directly. 

As qith any project working with agents, I was going to start by defining some instruction files (e.g. AGENTS.md), but instead of writing those myself, I pointed the agents at the principles repo, negotiated what we needed, and they wrote the files themselves, with the principles built in. What grew from there was so organic! A way of working that kept improving itself, with every lesson written down for the next session. The side effect that surprised me most: my agents kept me closer to my own principles.

At the start of any project, everyone agrees on good practices. Two weeks later, busy with the work, we forget. Human attention is limited. Discipline is expensive and sometimes it seems not feasible. Here I saw the opposite. My agents reread the rules every time, in seconds. They stick to the rules. Moreover, when I drifted, they pushed back. They kept me accountable to my own decisions.

This was the first real test of my principles. We effectively externalized the project memory and its processes: Reading is remembering.

Same four pillars as last time, now with real examples:
I. Think before you generate: the backlog became a contract.
II. Generate responsibly: review went every direction. AI reviewed AI, I reviewed the AI, and the AI reviewed me.
III. Prove it works: assumptions were labeled "not proven yet" until we tested them.
IV. Build for the future: core docs, written once, for humans and AIs alike.

None of that is new. What has changed is what it costs to follow it.

What failed, failed where systems meet: a line I missed in an API's docs, and a file a tool said it saved but didn't. Agents move faster than people can absorb, so my next experiment is to slow down at the boundaries.

In my first post I asked: how much engineering discipline can we afford? Turns out, quite a lot! And the speed of development was exceptional even with the engineering discipline. Alone, I'd probably still be setting up the repo 😅.

Want to try it yourself? Point your agents at the principles repo, and let the cooking begin.
